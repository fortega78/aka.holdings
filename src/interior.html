What we do differently

Plenty of studios make beautiful rooms. These five habits are ours — and each one has a project behind it, not a promise.

I

We enter through the art.

The first question is never the sofa — it is the piece the home will be built around. In Coconut Cove that was a Roberto Matta from 1969; in the collector’s apartment, twenty works that only needed to be seen properly.

{{fig:colf-entry-wall|A wall of framed works over a bronze sculpture in the collector’s apartment|The collector’s apartment: the art decides, everything else follows.}}
II

Every piece comes with its reason, in writing.

Not a mood board — a list where each piece carries the argument for its presence: why this red, why chrome, why nothing else on the table. You approve reasons, not vibes.

{{fig:colf-bronze|The dining table with caned chairs and the gold bronze alone at its centre|From the collector’s apartment list: “the bronze sits alone at its centre.”}}
III

The plan is verified before a dollar is spent.

We rebuild the architect’s plan in centimetres and place every piece to scale — clearances, door swings, rug sizes. In Quito that meant six dining chairs instead of eight, caught on paper, not in the freight elevator.

{{fig:qtf-plan-final|The final furnished plan of the Quito apartment, every piece numbered|Quito: the final plan, verified in centimetres.}}
IV

We can make the piece that doesn’t exist.

Through AKA Art Productions we work with master craftspeople in eight countries — so when the right work cannot be bought, it can be made. For Quito we designed a felt triptych and sized it to the wall.

{{fig:qtf-felt-b|Centre panel of the felt triptych designed by AKA: quilted cream wool with a brown circuit-like figure|Render — the centre panel of the Quito felt work.}}
V

One person, from the first plan to the last book.

Measuring, proposal, trade purchasing, deliveries, installers, millwork and the final styling — one interlocutor for all of it. Edgewater ran this way, end to end — to the photograph above.

{{fig:edgf-kitchen|Kitchen bar with stools under a globe chandelier, a wine wall in oak and a bouclé armchair|Edgewater: the kitchen bar, the wine wall built into the millwork.}}

How we work

  1. ConsultationAt your home or online. We measure, listen, and you leave with a written plan: what to keep, what to move, what to buy and in what order.
  2. ProposalA furnished plan to scale, the palette and every piece with the reason it is there. You approve it before anything is spent.
  3. PurchasingThrough our trade accounts with more than ten furniture, lighting and textile brands. You see the retail price next to yours.
  4. InstallationDeliveries, installers and millwork coordinated by us, and the home styled down to the last book.

What you can ask us for

  • A consultationFor a home that almost works: one visit and a written plan of what to keep, move and buy.
  • One roomFurnished plan, palette, render and the list of every piece at designer pricing.
  • Remote designFor homes outside Miami: we design, buy online and coordinate shipping — the way Quito was designed.
  • The whole homeConstruction, millwork, furniture and art, with one point of contact from the first plan to the last book.

Fees depend on the size of the home and the scope; we propose them after the first conversation. Furniture and art are bought at designer pricing.
