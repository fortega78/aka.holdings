A collector’s apartment, Miami

Collection styling. Proposal, 2026.

More than twenty works in one open room. Nothing leaves the collection and nothing is added to it. The proposal rearranges and lights the art, and changes only what sits around it: a few pieces with a signature, warm textures, and one colour taken from the paintings.

The photographs show the apartment as it is today, before the proposal.

What the proposal adds, and why

Spaghetti vaseGaetano Pesce, Fish Design. Red resin, made by hand.
The only object on the pine coffee table, next to two stacked monographs. Its red is the red of the tiger in the painting above.
Panthella 160 lampVerner Panton, Louis Poulsen. Chrome.
On the amber cart under the painting, with a candle in amber glass. Chrome is the only new metal, next to the cantilever chairs.
Bilboquet table lampsPhilippe Malouin, Flos. Linen.
On both sides of the bed, with a head that turns to the page or to the wall.
ParentesiAchille and Pier Giacomo Castiglioni, Flos, 1971.
If the lounge chair becomes a reading corner: a cable from ceiling to floor with a single spot, almost no volume.
Loop candleholdersItalian, vintage, silver-plated, in the manner of Lino Sabattini.
Two, on the diagonal, on each side of the bronze: the first thing you see when you walk in.
Oxide plantersTen Thousand Pots. Glazed ceramic, made by hand.
A glaze from chocolate to rust, the colour of the pine table and the leather chair.
Picture lightsOne family, polished chrome.
The same light for every work that needs one, so the eye goes to the art, not the fixtures.

Design and curation: AKA Interior Design, Fabian Ortega. The artists of the collection are not named.
