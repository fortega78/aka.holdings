Archivo Líquido: twelve meters of silk, embroidered by hand.

The making of the work (PDF)

Carlos Amorales, Archivo Líquido, 2026. One of the longest suzanis ever created as a contemporary artwork, and the first at this scale by a Latin American artist that we have been able to document: twelve meters of silk embroidery by 36 women in Uzbekistan, with the artist’s drawing running unbroken across every seam.

The work is the central piece of La vía de la máscara, Carlos Amorales’ first exhibition in Peru, curated by Cuauhtémoc Medina at AMIL, Lima, on view until February 6, 2027.

Silk was always the goal. From 2018 we wrote to, called and visited more than twenty-five groups of artisans, looking for someone able to embroider silk at this scale. In 2022 we found the workshop of Bahodir A. and his daughter Aisha A. in Uzbekistan, who weave their own cloth and raise their own silkworms.

The seam

A tradition, changed at one point.

A historic suzani was drawn across four to six narrow strips of cloth. The strips were then separated, embroidered by different women of a family and sewn back together, which is why the drawing so often fails to meet at the seams.

For Archivo Líquido the workshop joined the strips first, traced the complete drawing onto the joined cloth, a month of work, and never separated them again. Thirty-six women embroidered it on nine wooden frames at once, the rest of the twelve meters rolled at the frame’s edge. The line runs unbroken across every seam.

  1. 01SilkSilkworms raised by the workshop; thread twisted by hand. King Silk from the Fergana Valley.
  2. 02BlackThe thread is dyed by hand, built up to black through successive indigo baths.
  3. 03GroundA dense cloth, silk on the face and cotton on the back, woven by the workshop in narrow widths.
  4. 04DrawingThe strips are joined and Carlos Amorales’ drawing is traced by hand across all twelve meters.
  5. 05EmbroideryBigiz saima, the hook stitch of the Fergana Valley: 36 women, nine frames, from October to December 2025.

Eight years to a single work.

Since the late 1990s Carlos Amorales has built the Archivo Líquido, a repertoire of silhouettes from which much of his work derives. With AKA Art Productions it has taken textile form in Kathmandu, Kyrgyzstan and Mali, and now in Uzbekistan.
