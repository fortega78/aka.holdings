Edgewater Residence, Miami.

Project 02

Oceanfront Apartment, Ecuador.

Ecuador coast · 2018 · Our own home · Completed

An apartment over the Pacific, designed and furnished by Fabian himself, room by room. White linen sofas held in solid red-wood blocks, a marble table on a crowd of oak legs, and a wall of geometric works that spills across the plaster. One wall is painted in an unfinished stroke of blue, so the room never looks done.

Over the dining table, a cluster of bare bulbs on long black cables; the table itself is a patchwork of oak blocks under glass, facing the sea. In the bedroom, the bed is framed by four tree trunks. On the roof, a pergola of cane over a floor of patterned tiles.

Project 03

Apartment with Antiques, Ecuador.

Ecuador · 2018 · Our own home · Completed

The opposite of the beach: a layered home over the river where family antiques — a carved sideboard, a bracket clock, crystal candelabra — live with modern pieces. Draped, tufted sofas face each other across a chevron hide, around a table of brass and glass.

A dining room for twelve under a large black-and-blue work, a bar painted black around a portrait, and a study brushed in blue across walls and ceiling like a woven check. Every room is anchored by its rug.

Project 04

Collector’s Apartment, Miami.

Miami · 2026 · Collection styling · Proposal

More than twenty works in an open-plan apartment. The brief: nothing leaves, nothing is added to the collection. It is rearranged and lit, and the layer around it is rewritten — few pieces with a signature, warm textures, and a single thread of colour taken from the art.

Collection styling · lighting for the art · a written list of every piece

The pieces, and why they are there

Spaghetti vaseGaetano Pesce · Fish Design · red resin, made by hand

The only object on the pine coffee table, next to two stacked monographs. Its red is the red of the tiger.

Panthella 160, chromeVerner Panton · Louis Poulsen

On the amber cart under the tiger, with a candle in amber glass: a warm altar beneath the painting. Chrome and silver are the only new metal, in conversation with the cantilever chairs.

Bilboquet table lampsPhilippe Malouin · Flos · linen

At both sides of the bed, with a head that turns to the page or to the wall.

ParentesiCastiglioni · Flos · 1971

If the lounge chair becomes a reading corner: a cable from ceiling to floor with a single spot, almost no volume.

Loop candleholdersItalian, vintage, silver-plated · in the manner of Lino Sabattini

Two, on the diagonal, flanking the bronze — the first thing you see when you walk in.

Oxide plantersTen Thousand Pots · glazed ceramic, made by hand

A glaze that runs from chocolate to rust, in the colour of the pine table and the leather of the lounge chair: it reads as studio ceramics, not as a nursery pot.

Picture lightsone family, polished chrome

A single family of lights for the works that need them, so the collection — not the fixtures — is what you see.

Project 05

Quito Residence, Ecuador.

Quito · 2026 · Remote curation · Proposal

A family apartment in Quito, designed from Miami and furnished at a distance: a plan rebuilt from the architect’s drawings, every piece with its own sheet and a block that says why it is there, with online purchasing and shipping to Ecuador. Twenty-four people can sit at once, in four groups that see each other.

Plan · furniture and art · a piece made to order · purchasing and shipping

Six chairs, not eight: comfort rules.

The Klub chair by Steen Østergaard is 63.5 cm wide; three a side would overlap by 2.5 cm. Two a side plus two heads at the table, and four more at the island.

A piece made for the dining wall.

“Trama Topográfica”: a wool-felt work drawn by the studio for the wall behind the table, in the shyrdak felt tradition — one continuous line that enters, turns and leaves. In prototype.

A work over the only quiet corner.

A Kathmandu paper wall work by Jessica Stockholder, in burgundy, chosen against the palette: it completes the red thread of a Rosso Levanto marble side table and balances the two greens.

A rug that stays quiet.

On the terrace, a flat weave that takes the whole seating area and stops 34 cm short of the fire — the fire is never over a rug.

How we work

Four steps, one point of contact.

  1. 1ConsultationAt your home or online. We measure, listen and leave with scope and priorities: what to keep, what to move, what to buy and in what order.
  2. 2ProposalA furnished plan to scale, the palette and every piece with its reason. You approve it before spending.
  3. 3PurchasingThrough our trade accounts at more than ten furniture, lighting and textile brands: you see the retail price next to yours.
  4. 4Installation & stylingDeliveries, installers and millwork coordinated, and the home styled down to the last book.

Design tools we build ourselves.

Our sister company, AKA Technologies, builds the tools the studio works with. Plans are rebuilt from the architect’s drawings and checked automatically — walls, clearances, door swings, the sofa inside the rug — before a single piece is bought.

Sourcing runs across a fixed bank of stores with dimensions and dated prices, and palettes are sampled from the actual pieces, not guessed. Concept images are always labelled as concepts.

About

Fabian Ortega.

I was born in Ecuador, Miami has been home since I was a kid, and I studied at the University of Miami. I have designed and furnished my own homes in Ecuador and Miami since 2018. Through AKA Art Productions I produce work with contemporary artists and master craftspeople in Nepal, Kyrgyzstan, India, Uzbekistan, Mali and Mexico, shown at Art Basel Miami Beach, Art Basel Paris, NADA Miami and Museo Jumex.

That is where the studio’s eye comes from. I lead every project myself, from the consultation to the installation, in English and Spanish.
