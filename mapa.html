Why a map first

Why a map first

I have spent more than twenty years operating businesses — a $100M vehicle distributor with a thousand people, a franchise network of more than a hundred locations, a hospitality platform of two hundred units, and today the four businesses of AKA. In every one of them, the expensive problems were never where the software demo pointed.

They were in handoffs nobody owned, in data that arrived too late to matter, in decisions that had an outcome but no owner. So before we ever talk about AI, we make a map.

— Fabian J. Ortega A., founder

How it works

The route: four phases, each closed by a gate nobody skips.

Including us. Open each phase for what happens, what you take away, and the gate that closes it. The detail — every instrument and template — lives in the Manual.

MMap the lossWhere does this business lose value — measured, not assumed?
What we do

We interrogate the work as it actually happens, not as the org chart says it happens: six questions, pushed past the symptom until the answer names the condition producing the problem. Every loss gets coordinates on the nine elements where work can break — from roles and handoffs to data, timing and accountability — and a confidence state. Missing evidence never stops the map and never gets invented: it is logged as a gap, with what it blocks and who unblocks it.

What you take away

The loss map: one page per loss — its cost, its coordinates, who suffers it, since when — plus the gap register.

The gate

Every loss has a figure or is honestly marked as a question. Nothing is “estimated.”

Compass bearing

Framing — what problem, for whom?

AAssign an ownerWho owns each loss — with the authority to change what produces it?
What we do

Every loss gets one named owner — and we check the thing most diagnostics skip: whether that person has the authority to change what produces the loss. A target being met is not proof the system works; it is often the measure of someone’s heroics compensating for it. Both the authority gaps and the masking workarounds go on record, and the decision ledger opens: every decision the engagement takes or defers gets an ID, an owner, a deadline and the cost of not deciding.

What you take away

The owner table — one named person per loss, their real authority, the workarounds found — and the opened decision ledger.

The gate

No loss without an owner. If nobody can own it, we don’t build anything for it.

Compass bearing

Accounting — who answers for it?

PPlan the workHow should the work happen — with or without AI — and which work first?
What we do

Before any tool is named, we redesign the work on paper and ask what capability the problem actually requires — description, explanation, prediction, recommendation, or generation. Many losses close right here, without AI: a sequence fix, a role fix. And because hours are the scarcest resource in an operator-led company, nothing gets ours — or yours — on a hunch: every candidate intervention is scored on our own impact model before it starts, ranked by expected value, and committed to in writing with its metric, its baseline and its kill criterion set in advance. Three initiatives active at most; one income priority at a time.

What you take away

The redesigned work on paper, plus the scored, ranked portfolio — at most three committed initiatives, pre-registered.

The gate

The owner approves the redesign before a single tool is chosen — and no score, no hours.

Compass bearing

Orienting — what capability?

AAutomate under rulesWhat runs, what does it decide alone, what stays with a person — and how will we know it worked?
What we do

Only now does AI enter, and it enters contained. We write the rulebook first: what the system may decide alone, what stays with a person, what it must never touch, what gets logged, and what fails closed. Any step that would combine untrusted input, sensitive data and the power to act on the world gets a human in the loop — no exceptions. Every automated action starts low on an autonomy ladder and earns each level with evidence; anything irreversible never runs without approval.

What you take away

The rulebook: every action classified into zones, the approval contract, the kill switches, the blocked list, and the success metrics with their baselines.

The gate

The rules are written and approved before the first agent runs. No exceptions — our own systems included.

Compass bearing

Judging and accounting — is it working, and who answers?

After the route: verified operation.

The system lives under the same compass we used to build it, reports in three states, and its autonomy moves one level at a time — in both directions. Autonomy is earned, never assumed.

The compass

Governance is not a gate at the end — it is a thread through the whole engagement. Four readings, each a question with a named answer.

Honest terms

What we need from you — and what we don’t do.

A diagnostic is only as honest as its access. These terms are why the map comes back true.

We need
  • Access to the real work and the people who do it — not only the org chart.
  • An owner with the authority to decide, on your side.
  • The data you already have, even if it is messy. Finding out what it is worth is part of the map.
We don’t do
  • Tool demos before the map.

  • Automating the irreversible without explicit approval.
The rules of navigation

The frameworks are taught; the rules are earned.

Fourteen operating rules, written by correcting real mistakes in our own operations. Anyone can list four phases — these are what we run them with.

The fourteen rules
  • 01Precedence of sources. Measured beats system data beats mail beats inference.
  • 02Intention ≠ fact. Scheduled is not done — for people and for agents alike.
  • 03Three states, always visible. Verified · last known · open question. A question never enters a sum.
  • 04One loss, one owner. No owner, no build.
  • 05The work before the tool. The redesign is approved before a tool is named.
  • 06Rules before the first agent. The rulebook is written and approved before anything runs.
  • 07The autonomy ladder. 0–4, one level at a time, both directions. Irreversible caps at 2.
  • 08The decision ledger. Every decision gets an ID, an owner, a deadline and the cost of not deciding.
  • 09Contradictions get a closing date. Never averaged — registered, owned, closed.
  • 10Rhythm. Daily · weekly with at most three priorities · close. The ritual reads the pre-registered metrics.
  • 11The gap register. Missing evidence never stops the work and never gets invented.
  • 12Pre-registration. Metric, baseline, kill criterion — before launch, never retroactively.
  • 13The two-currency budget. Hours and capital are different currencies; in an operator-led company, hours bind.
  • 14Anti-babysitting. If approvals eat more than ~10 minutes a day, the calibration is wrong — recalibrate by a logged decision, never by bypassing.

Questions owners ask first

Asked before every map.

What if AI isn’t the answer for my business?

Then the map says so, in writing, and shows what to fix first without it. Many of the losses we find close with a sequence fix or a role fix — no tool at all. That finding costs less than the tool would have.

Do I need clean data before we start?

No. Part of the map is finding out what data you actually have, where it lives and who owns it. Messy data is a finding, not a blocker.

Are you going to sell me software?

The map stands on its own and is yours to act on with anyone. If the route points to a system, we can build it — but nothing we build enters before the map exists, by our own rule.

Why not just use platform X?

Own the brain, rent the muscles. The parts that determine the outcome — strategy, rules, measurement, memory — you should own; commodity capabilities you rent. A rented decision layer aligns your company to the industry average; an owned one aligns it to your business.

How do you treat our data?

The rules are written before anything runs: what may be seen, by whom, hard separation between businesses, and a person approving anything irreversible. We run our own four businesses under the same rules.

How long does it take, and what does it cost?

Scope, duration and fee are agreed in writing before we start, sized to your business. The first call is where we set them.

Can we work in Spanish?

Yes — the map, the documents and the meetings, in English, Spanish or both.
