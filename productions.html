AKA Art Productions

Made with the artist, by hand.

Since a first exploration in Peru in 2007, we have travelled with contemporary artists to the places where a technique is still alive — Ecuador, Kyrgyzstan, Nepal, Mali, India, Uzbekistan — and produced the work there, by hand, with the master craftspeople who keep it.

00Peru and Ecuador · 2007–2018

Before the studio: reed, paper and straw.

The exploration began in Peru in 2007, among the reed boats of the coast — caballitos de totora, built by hand the same way for centuries.

In 2018, in Ecuador, Vargas-Suárez Universal spent days drawing on paper by the sea at Punta Barandúa, and in Manabí we went to the weavers of paja toquilla, the fine straw of the Panama hat. Each trip asked the same question: what can an artist make here, by hand, that could not be made anywhere else?

01Kyrgyzstan · 2018

Bishkek: an artist’s drawing, pressed into felt.

In September 2018 we made our first trip to Kyrgyzstan, to the felt-makers of ala-kiyiz and shyrdak — wool laid, wetted, rolled and pressed by hand, one of the oldest textile techniques in Central Asia. It was where AKA’s work with artists truly began.

The first project was with Vargas-Suárez Universal: his drawings were laid out full size on the workshop table, and a master felt-maker built them up in black and white wool, sample by sample, until the felt could hold every line.

02New York · 2019

The work comes to New York.

In 2019 the textile works we produced with Vargas-Suárez Universal were shown in New York, in an exhibition curated by Carla Stellweg in her own space: large pieces hung wall to wall, in the rooms where people live with art. One of them went on to hang in the double-height lobby of a new building in Manhattan.

The same year we visited Saint Clair Cemin’s studio in Brooklyn and began a textile collaboration with him.

03Kyrgyzstan · 2019

Back to the felt: Cemin, Bedia, Vargas-Suárez.

In 2019 we went back to Kyrgyzstan with new drawings. The felt-makers built them in ala-kiyiz — loose wool laid by colour on the table, then wetted, rolled and pressed until it binds into a single cloth.

For Saint Clair Cemin, a figure in blue, yellow and black taken from his painting on paper. For José Bedia, his line drawings in black wool, finished by hand stitch by stitch. For Vargas-Suárez Universal, the clouded blue of the work that later hung in New York.

04Nepal · 2019–2021

Kathmandu: paper made by the artists’ own hands.

Lokta paper is made in the Himalayan foothills from the inner bark of the lokta bush. We brought artists to Kathmandu to work at the vats with the papermakers. Jessica Stockholder made her own sheets and worked into them — tearing, layering, adding colour — learning the paper as she went. Vargas-Suárez Universal worked in paper too. Aya Takano, on the same trip, worked on a textile piece hand-knotted in Nepal.

Stockholder’s works were later shown by her galleries; Takano’s series was shown in Paris, Tokyo and Hong Kong. In 2021, hand-knotted tapestries we produced in Kathmandu for Carlos Amorales and Dr. Lakra were shown by kurimanzutto in East Hampton.

05Films · 2021–2023

Two documentaries on art, craft and place.

The work in the workshops became two films: one on the papermakers of Nepal, one on the culture of Georgia.

Lokta Kingdom

A feature documentary that follows one of the last families making lokta paper in Nepal, and the community determined to keep the craft alive. Directed by Andy Fabrykant; photography by Martin Quirno; executive producer Fabian J. Ortega. Nepal, 2022 · 88 minutes · in Nepali.

Official Selection · Nepal Cultural International Film Festival 2023

Latin Nomads

A documentary filmed in Tbilisi and Mtskheta, made to bring Georgia’s art and culture to a wider audience — with figures from the country’s public and cultural life, from a former head of state to its leading chef.

06Kyrgyzstan → Art Basel · 2022–2024

Carlos Amorales, in felt.

Four years after the first trip, the felt-makers made Carlos Amorales’ first piece in Kyrgyz felt, in May 2022. The series that followed brings ala-kiyiz and shyrdak — pressed and cut wool — to the scale of a painting.

Jungle Star (Universe), 289 × 203 cm, was shown by kurimanzutto at Art Basel Miami Beach, 4–8 December 2024. Other works in the series have been shown at international art fairs.

07Mali · 2024

Bogolan: cloth painted with mud.

Bogolanfini is Mali’s mud cloth: cotton dyed with plants, then painted, line by line, with fermented mud. With artisans in Mali we translated Carlos Amorales’ forms into the technique.

Dreamachine, cotton painted with mud, 160 × 120 cm, was shown at Albarrán Bourdais, Madrid, in 2024.

08India and Nepal · 2024–2025

Mother of pearl, hair and black stone.

In Udaipur, craftspeople cut mother of pearl and abalone into small tiles and set them one by one on a drawn grid. With Gabriel de la Mora we produced mother-of-pearl works there, and hanging works in human hair, 90 × 60 cm; his human-hair works were shown at Museo Jumex.

In Nepal, stone carvers cut Dr. Lakra’s drawing — a monkey, a skull between two cobras, a crocodile on a lotus base — in black ballistic stone, working from the artist’s drawings and proportion guides, with revisions sent between studio and workshop.

09Uzbekistan · 2025–2026

Tejido Líquido: twelve metres of silk, by hand.

Silk was always the goal. From 2018 we wrote to, called and visited more than twenty-five groups of artisans, looking for someone able to embroider silk at the scale we had in mind. In 2022 we found the workshop of Bahodir and his daughter Aisha in Uzbekistan, who weave their own cloth and raise their own silkworms.

For Carlos Amorales we produced Tejido Líquido: a suzani of 1.80 × 12 m, embroidered in bigiz saima, the hook stitch of the Fergana Valley, with hand-dyed silk on silk-and-cotton cloth woven by the workshop. The black is built up from successive indigo baths. In the collections and sources we consulted, we have not found a suzani of comparable size.

How a production works

The artist leads. We translate the drawing into a technique and a workshop.

  1. 1ProposalThe artist’s drawing and intention, size and use.
  2. 2Technique & workshopWe match the work to the craft and to the master craftspeople who keep it alive.
  3. 3SamplesColour, material and density approved by the artist before production.
  4. 4ProductionMade by hand in the workshop, with documentation for each piece.
  5. 5DeliveryFinishing, shipping and delivery handled end to end.
Crafts

Eight techniques passed down for generations.

Hand-knotting

Nepal. Tibetan knots in Himalayan wool, with silk and yak hair.

Suzani embroidery

Uzbekistan. Hand embroidery in silk on silk-and-cotton atlas.

Felt

Kyrgyzstan. Shyrdak and ala-kiyiz, in Tien Shan wool.

Bogolan

Mali. Cotton dyed with plants and painted with fermented mud.

Lokta paper

Nepal. Paper from the bark of the lokta bush, made by the artists.

Mother of pearl and abalone

India. Inlay set by hand, tile by tile.

Stone

Nepal. Sculpture carved by hand; jade mosaic in development in Mexico and India.

Hair

India. Works in human hair with Gabriel de la Mora.
